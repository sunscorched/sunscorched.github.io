"Polygonal billiards is the study of Newtonian motion of a point inside a polygon." - Paul Seidel. We typically assume that all collisions are completely inelastic and there is no friction so that the trajectory of the ball can go on indefinitely. When the ball hits a wall, it comes in at some angle measured between the trajectory line and the wall. We have some choices here so we'll always choose to work with an angle $\alpha$ between 0 and $\pi/2$. The ball then reflects off of the wall such that the angle of the outgoing ray and the wall is also $\alpha$.
